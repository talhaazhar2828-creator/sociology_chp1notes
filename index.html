Introduction to Sociology

Definitions · Ibn Khaldun · Auguste Comte · Imraniyat

What is Sociology?

From Latin socius (companion/society) + Greek logos (study) — literally "the study of society."

Sociology is the scientific study of social life, society, human life and culture, social interaction, social behaviour, and social institutions.

Other useful definitions: Durkheim — the science of social facts that shape individual behaviour. Weber — the interpretive understanding of social action. Giddens — the scientific study of human social life, groups and societies.

Auguste Comte 1838

French philosopher who coined the term "Sociology" and is called the Father of Sociology. Believed society could be studied with the same scientific methods as physics — he first wanted to call it "Social Physics." His Law of Three Stages: Theological → Metaphysical → Positive (scientific).

Ibn Khaldun 1332–1406

Muslim historian whose book Al-Muqaddimah studied society scientifically 400 years before Comte. Called it Imraniyat (Ilm al-Umran — "science of civilization"). Introduced Asabiyyah, social solidarity that drives the rise and fall of civilizations. Seen as sociology's pioneer/forerunner.

"Man is a social animal"

Traced to Aristotle ("zoon politikon"). Humans can't fully develop in isolation — we need groups, relationships and culture to survive. This idea is the philosophical root of why sociology exists as a science of groups, not individuals.

Subject Matter of Sociology

10 areas sociology studies or applies

1 · Gender Study

How society constructs roles, values, limits and needs differently for men and women, and works on inequality/rights.

2 · Social Work

The helping profession — applies social knowledge to assist individuals, families and communities directly (counselling, case work, welfare support).

3 · Criminology

Studies why crime happens (poverty, unemployment, broken families), types of crime, and prevention/control through police, courts and rehabilitation.

4 · Community Development

A group working together, bottom-up, to solve shared problems — clean water, sanitation, roads — often with NGO/government support.

5 · Civics & Community Engagement

Rights and duties of citizens: voting, paying taxes, obeying laws, and active participation in community/local governance.

6 · Sociology of Religion

How religion shapes society's values and rules — and how society shapes religious practice in turn.

7 · Sociology of Education

Two-way link: how class/family/policy affect access to education, and how education shapes society (mobility, values, skills).

8 · Socialization

Lifelong process of learning a society's norms and values. Primary (family, early childhood), secondary (school, peers), re-socialization (adapting to a new environment, e.g. army/new country).

9 · Research Methodology

Qualitative — words, opinions, meaning (interviews, case studies). Quantitative — numbers, statistics (surveys, measurable data).

Steps: Survey → Interview → Data Collection → Analysis → Results

10 · Project Planning & Management

Designing, resourcing, implementing and evaluating social development projects — planning, budgeting, monitoring, and measuring outcomes (M&E).

Sociology as a Science

Why it counts, and where it's debated

What is science?

Systematic observation + collection of data, split into Natural Science (physics, biology) and Social Science (sociology, economics, psychology).

The research cycle

Survey → Interview → Data Collection → Analysis → Results

This is why sociology qualifies as a science: it doesn't guess — it gathers evidence, analyzes it, and reaches objective conclusions.

For

  • Systematic, objective methods
  • Finds real patterns (e.g. Durkheim's suicide studies)
  • Empirical, evidence-based

Against

  • Human behaviour less predictable
  • Full objectivity is hard
  • Controlled experiments are limited
